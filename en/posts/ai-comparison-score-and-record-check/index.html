Why Did These Results Occur?

The pitfalls in the three supplied problems are straightforward to explain.

In the first problem, grouping the steps in pairs shows that the score increases by 3 per pair. After calculation 18, the score is 27. Adding 4 at step 19 brings it to 31, so that is where the process stops. Waiting until after calculation 20 to check the score misses the instruction to stop in the middle of the pair.

The key to the second problem is avoiding double-counting overlapping elimination conditions. Removing the even numbers leaves 50 odd-numbered participants. Of these, 10 have numbers with 9 in the ones place. There are 5 odd numbers with 9 in the tens place, but 99 has already been counted. Thus, 14 odd-numbered participants are eliminated, leaving 36 people.

Test questions on screen (video screenshot)

In the third problem, a = [[]] * 3 does not create three separate empty lists. All three slots in the outer list refer to the same inner list. Appending 1 to that list makes all three slots appear as [1].

These explanations do not, however, account for the large numbers or long output in the actual responses, because the questions and responses do not line up. Claude's explanation of the 97 condition is also difficult to verify in full without the original problem statement.

The issue identified here is a mismatch between the comparison materials. We must also distinguish between simply giving a wrong number and explicitly stating that an exact answer could not be produced. Claude's recorded FAIL is the latter.

Conclusion

The following sequence is useful when checking AI answers at work.

  • Confirm that the original question and the response belong to the same problem.
  • Compare the answer key and the actual submitted values side by side.
  • When an exact number is required, distinguish between an approximation and declining to give a definitive answer.
  • Run code with the same input, and check intermediate values in calculations.
  • Mark tests that were not run separately from wrong answers.

The conditions in this record were 1 attempt and 3 problems. The three-way comparison in Set 4 is incomplete because Gemini was not run. The mismatch between questions and responses also means that the recorded scores cannot be independently verified or generalized into a broader ranking of the models.

What the next comparison needs first is not a ranking table, but a clear connection between the questions, correct answers, and submitted responses. Scores become meaningful only once we establish that we are looking at the same problems.
